Basic Instructions

Here is where users will learn about terminology used in patterns and how to begin crocheting. Terminology is typically clarified in patterns, but to familiarize, some context will be provided here.

Abbreviations

Abbreviations are used in patterns to save space and make instructions more concise. Here are some common crochet abbreviations:

  • mr/mc - magic ring/magic circle
  • ch - chain
  • sc - single crochet
  • hdc - half double crochet
  • dc - double crochet
  • inc - increase
  • dec - decrease
  • sl st - slip stitch
  • st(s) - stitch(es)
  • yo - yarn over

Basic Steps

Here is how you will learn to do the basic steps that will allow you to begin crocheting.

Slip knot

To make a slip knot, you make a simple loop with your yarn and pull the working yarn through the hole and pulling to secure the knot and adjust the size of it.

Yarn Over

To yarn over, you wrap the yarn over your hook from back to front. This is a fundamental step in many crochet stitches.

Magic Ring

To make a magic ring, you wrap the yarn around your fingers to create a loop, insert your hook into the loop, yarn over and pull up a loop, then chain one to secure the ring. You can then work stitches into the ring as needed.

Chain

To make a chain, you yarn over and pull through the loop on your hook. This is the foundation of most crochet projects.

Single Crochet

To make a single crochet, you insert your hook into the stitch, yarn over and pull up a loop, yarn over again and pull through both of the two loops on your hook.

Half Double Crochet

To make a half double crochet, you yarn over, insert your hook into the stitch, yarn over and pull up a loop, yarn over again and pull through all three loops on your hook.

Double Crochet

To make a double crochet, you yarn over, insert your hook into the stitch, yarn over and pull up a loop, yarn over again and pull through the first two loops on your hook, yarn over again and pull through the remaining two loops on your hook.

Slip Stitch

To make a slip stitch, you insert your hook into the stitch, yarn over and pull through both the stitch and the loop on your hook in one motion.

Increase

To make an increase, you work two stitches into the same stitch.

Decrease

To make a decrease, you work two stitches together. For example, to make a single crochet decrease, you insert your hook into the stitch, yarn over and pull up a loop, insert your hook into the next stitch, yarn over and pull up another loop, yarn over again and pull through all three loops on your hook.
